Raster Angle Effects on 3D-Printed CFR-PLA

Mechanical characterization and microstructural analysis of composite prints.

Project Overview

This experimental study investigated how the raster angle (the orientation of the deposited material strands) influences the flexural strength and failure mechanisms of Carbon Fiber Reinforced PLA (CFR-PLA). By combining mechanical testing with Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM), we established a clear correlation between print orientation and structural performance.

Experimental Design

  • Specimen Fabrication: Samples were printed using a high-precision FDM printer with raster angles of 0°, 45°, and 90°.
  • Mechanical Testing: Performed three-point bending tests according to ASTM D790 standards to determine flexural modulus and peak strength.
  • Microstructural Analysis: Utilized Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) to examine fracture surfaces, focusing on fiber-matrix adhesion and void distribution.
  • Data Analysis: Processed raw load-displacement data to calculate average strength and standard deviation across multiple specimens.

Key Results & Analysis

25% Strength Increase (0° vs 45°)
ASTM D790 Testing Protocol
SEM Micro-Failure Mode Identified

Analysis of the data revealed that a 0° raster angle provides the highest flexural strength due to the alignment of carbon fibers with the principal stress direction. SEM imagery confirmed that delamination was the primary failure mode for 45° and 90° samples, whereas fiber breakage predominated in 0° samples.
