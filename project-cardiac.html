Ongoing Research

Customized 3D Cardiac Models

Advanced tissue engineering for patient-specific cardiovascular solutions.

Project Overview

This ongoing research focuses on the development of patient-specific cardiac models through 3D bioprinting. By utilizing decellularized Extracellular Matrix (dECM) based bio-inks, we aim to create biomimetic scaffolds that accurately replicate the mechanical and biochemical environment of the human heart. This project has significant implications for personalized drug testing and surgical planning.

Methodology & Technical Approach

  • Material Sourcing: Utilization of porcine-derived dECM to ensure biocompatibility and provide essential growth factors.
  • Bio-ink Formulation: Optimizing viscosity and cross-linking density to maintain structural integrity post-printing.
  • Bioprinting Parameters: Systematic evaluation of nozzle diameter, extrusion pressure, and print speed to achieve high-fidelity micro-architectures.
  • Characterization: Assessing rheological properties and mechanical strength (Young's modulus) to match native cardiac tissue.

Key Research Objectives

My work involves a comprehensive literature review and experimental design aimed at:

  • Improving the resolution of printed cardiac fibers to sub-100 micron scales.
  • Enhancing cell viability (>90%) within the bioprinted scaffolds through nutrient-rich bio-ink additives.
  • Developing a quantifiable metric for "printability" based on rheological data.

Current Progress

Currently, the project is in the initial phase of dECM processing and bio-ink standardization. Early characterization results show promising results in maintaining the natural collagen architecture required for cardiomyocyte alignment.
